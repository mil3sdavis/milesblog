Recently, I had an interview experience that didn't go great. I had interviewed with HR, and was passed on to the hiring manager. The hiring manager asked about a skill that I had very little experience in but I said that it was something I'd be willing to work on learning. She seemed impressed with me and had me interview with her team. 

The entire IT department at this organization was the size of the Information Security team at my previous job. The team seemed like they were specifically interested in hiring someone with this specific skill, but were framing questions about it pertaining to my most recent position which didn't touch on this skill at all and was something that was handled by an entirely different team at my old organization. 

It wasn't until a few days after the interview that I stared reflecting back on my previous experience with the DoD did I realize that I did indeed have some experience in that field, but in the DoD flavor of that skill. I worked in civil service the DoD for a decade and gained many skills that on the surface aren't immediately obvious how they may map to certain areas in the public sector. Skills that seem to only apply to the very specific rigid government way of doing things. 

Walking away from this experience, I see that many of my skills can be reframed and are applicable in ways that I hadn't initially realized.
